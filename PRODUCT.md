# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

ios

## Users

Cool Spot primarily serves people in London who are outdoors, travelling, or otherwise exposed to uncomfortable or unsafe heat. They may be tired, hurried, using the phone one-handed, or working with limited attention. Their main job is to find a nearby place that could help them cool down, understand why it is credible and suitable, and begin travelling there within 30 seconds.

Secondary users save places or exact locations for later, share time-specific experiences, and contribute stable cooling information to a reviewed public map. These activities must remain clearly distinct from one another so that private saving is never mistaken for a public claim and a recent visit is never mistaken for lasting place information.

## Product Purpose

Cool Spot helps people discover and assess reviewed places where they can cool down in London. It combines official and community-supplied place information, recent visitor experience, and short-lived anonymous presence without presenting any one signal as a guarantee of current conditions.

Success means a person can quickly answer three questions: Where can I go now? Why might this place help me? What evidence can I trust? They can then start directions without navigating through contribution or account features. Saving remains private; public information is submitted deliberately and reviewed before it changes the shared map.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, fresh, curious, and mutual-aid minded. The product should feel calm enough to trust under pressure, clear and refreshing rather than clinical, open to discovering useful places, and grounded in neighbours helping one another.

## References

- Apple Maps for familiar native map interaction, navigation patterns, and restrained interface chrome.
- Google Maps for separating place discovery, saving, reporting an experience, editing stable facts, and adding a missing place.
- iOS system conventions for accessibility, navigation, controls, typography, and motion.

## Anti-references

- A tourism or badge-collection app where exploration and rewards overshadow the immediate cooling need.
- A competitive leaderboard or engagement system that exploits heat, check-ins, or public contribution for gamification.
- A municipal database or government portal that exposes internal terminology, administrative structure, or unexplained acronyms.
- A generic SaaS dashboard assembled from repeated cards, decorative metrics, and interchangeable pastel surfaces.
- Interfaces that claim a place is verified, safe, available, or currently cool when the evidence only supports a narrower statement.

## Design Principles

1. **Reach a decision in 30 seconds.** Prioritise nearby options, decision-critical evidence, and directions over secondary product concepts.
   Clear information is the primary product value; collecting contributions is secondary. Reading visitor evidence and current-use information belongs to the decision task, while invitations to submit a report or share presence must not compete with it. A reasoned decision not to go because information is insufficient is also a valid outcome.
2. **Show what is known without overclaiming.** Keep source, stable cooling features, recent visitor experience, and live presence distinct; state unknown information explicitly.
3. **Keep private and public actions unmistakable.** Saving, visiting, checking in, and contributing must each describe their audience, duration, and consequence before commitment.
4. **Give every screen one dominant job.** Reveal secondary detail progressively and keep the primary action visually and semantically clear.
5. **Make mutual aid feel useful, not extractive.** Show how a contribution helps another person, minimise required effort, and reserve recognition for meaningful reviewed outcomes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Treat WCAG 2.2 AA contrast as the minimum visual baseline while following Apple Human Interface Guidelines for native behaviour. Support Dynamic Type without truncating or overlapping decision-critical content, provide meaningful VoiceOver labels and reading order, maintain at least 44 by 44 point touch targets, and never communicate status or selection through colour alone.

Support Dark Mode, Increase Contrast, Reduce Motion, and common colour-vision differences. Prefer plain, concrete language over acronyms and internal data-model terms. Design for heat stress, glare, fatigue, limited attention, and one-handed use: keep essential actions close, reduce memory demands, and do not require precision gestures for critical tasks.
